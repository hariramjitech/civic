AI-Powered Smart Infrastructure Monitoring and Civic Issue Management System
1. Executive Summary

The AI-Powered Smart Infrastructure Monitoring and Civic Issue Management System is a web-based platform designed to bridge the communication gap between citizens and government authorities regarding public infrastructure issues. Citizens frequently encounter problems such as potholes, road damage, water leakages, garbage accumulation, drainage blockages, power outages, broken streetlights, and damaged public assets. Existing reporting mechanisms are often fragmented, difficult to access, and lack transparency regarding resolution status.

This platform provides a centralized digital ecosystem where citizens can report infrastructure issues using photographs, geolocation data, and textual descriptions. Artificial Intelligence models automatically analyze uploaded images to classify issue types, estimate severity levels, identify duplicate complaints, and prioritize incidents. The platform then routes complaints to the appropriate government departments and tracks them throughout their lifecycle.

Authorities gain access to a comprehensive dashboard for monitoring complaints, assigning field officers, managing resolutions, generating reports, and analyzing trends. Advanced analytics and predictive maintenance capabilities enable municipalities to identify recurring problem areas and proactively address infrastructure risks before they become severe.

The proposed solution combines modern web technologies, machine learning, geospatial analysis, and real-time communication mechanisms to improve governance efficiency, increase citizen engagement, and support smart-city initiatives.

2. Project Vision

To create an intelligent, transparent, and scalable civic infrastructure management platform that empowers citizens to actively participate in urban governance while enabling government authorities to make data-driven decisions for infrastructure planning and maintenance.

3. Problem Statement

Government agencies receive thousands of infrastructure-related complaints annually. These complaints are often submitted through multiple channels, including phone calls, physical visits, emails, and separate departmental portals.

Major challenges include:

Lack of centralized complaint management
Duplicate complaint submissions
Slow response times
Limited transparency
Poor prioritization of critical issues
Lack of predictive maintenance capabilities
Difficulty in tracking responsible departments
Absence of citizen feedback mechanisms

The proposed system addresses these challenges through automation, AI-based analysis, GIS visualization, and real-time monitoring.
